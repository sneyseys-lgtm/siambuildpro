What does it actually cost to build a house in Phuket?

By SiamBuild Pro · May 2025 · 8 min read

This is the question we get asked more than any other. And it's a fair one — because finding a straight answer online is almost impossible. Most articles either give you a number that's years out of date, or hedge so much it's useless.

We're going to do something different: explain exactly what drives construction costs in Phuket, what the real variables are, and how to think about budgeting before you speak to a single contractor.

⚠️ Anyone who gives you a firm price per square metre without seeing your land, your design, and your specification is either guessing or selling you something. Costs vary enormously — and we'll explain why.

The five factors that determine your cost

1. The land itself

Before a single brick is laid, your land will have a significant effect on what the build costs. The key questions are:

  • Is it flat or sloped? A sloped plot requires retaining walls, more complex foundations, and additional drainage — all of which add cost. A dramatic hilltop view often comes with a dramatic foundation bill.
  • What's the soil like? Soft or sandy soil requires deeper or more complex foundations. In some parts of Phuket, you'll hit bedrock quickly. In others, you'll need pile foundations that add significant cost.
  • How accessible is it? A narrow road or remote location means higher logistics costs for materials and equipment. This is often overlooked in early budgeting.
  • What services are connected? Electricity, water, sewerage — if these need to be brought to the plot, that's additional cost that has nothing to do with the building itself.

2. The design and specification

The design is where most of the budget decisions are made — often before anyone has thought about budget at all. A simple rectangular single-storey house with a standard roof costs far less to build than a complex multi-level villa with cantilevers, large spans, and a flat roof that needs to handle tropical rainfall.

Specification covers everything inside: the quality of tiles, the kitchen, bathroom fittings, windows, doors, ceiling heights, the air conditioning system. Two houses with identical floor plans can have construction costs that differ by 50% or more based purely on specification choices.

This is why a good architect — one who understands construction costs, not just aesthetics — is worth every baht. Decisions made at design stage lock in a large portion of your budget.

3. Materials — local vs imported

Thailand has excellent local building materials, and using them sensibly keeps costs down. But many clients — particularly those building to European or Australian standards — want specific materials that have to be imported. Imported tiles, European kitchen fittings, specific window systems: these can multiply the cost of those line items several times over.

There's no right answer here. Local materials used well, with proper engineering behind them, will produce a house that performs excellently in the Thai climate. But if you have specific requirements, factor the import costs in early.

One area where we always recommend spending properly: waterproofing and drainage. Thailand gets serious rainfall. The places where cheap materials fail first — and most expensively — are always roofs, wet areas, and drainage systems.

4. Labour and project management

Labour costs in Thailand are lower than in Western countries, but they vary considerably depending on the skill level you're hiring. There's a significant difference between:

  • General unskilled labour for basic work
  • Skilled tradespeople — tilers, electricians, plumbers
  • Specialist subcontractors for structural work, MEP systems, pool construction
  • Project management and engineering oversight

The last item is where many clients try to save money — and where cutting corners is most expensive. A project without proper technical oversight will have problems. They might not show up immediately, but they will show up.

We put a certified engineer on every project specifically because the cost of fixing mistakes after the fact — in a tropical climate, where things move fast — is always higher than the cost of getting it right the first time.

5. Permits, fees, and the process

Building legally in Thailand requires permits. The process varies by location, plot type, and building type, and it takes time. Permit costs themselves are not enormous, but delays cost money — and projects that skip the permit process can face serious consequences, including demolition orders.

For foreign buyers, there are additional legal considerations around land ownership that affect how a project is structured. This is a separate topic — but one worth understanding before you buy land.

What about renovation?

Renovation costs are even harder to generalise than new build costs, because they depend entirely on what you're starting with. A condo that just needs cosmetic updating costs very differently from one that needs a full rewire, new plumbing, and structural work.

The most common mistake in renovation budgeting is underestimating what's inside the walls. Once you open things up, you often find that the electrical system isn't up to standard, the waterproofing has failed, or the structure has issues. A proper survey before work starts — not just a visual inspection — saves money in the long run.

💡 The honest answer to "how much will it cost?" is: we need to see your land, understand your brief, and assess your current situation (for renovation). Only then can we give you a real number. Anyone who quotes you a firm price without that information is guessing.

How to approach budgeting

Here's a practical approach that works:

  • Start with your total budget, not your build cost. Your total project cost includes land, design fees, permits, construction, landscaping, pool, furniture, and contingency. Many people only think about construction and are surprised by everything else.
  • Build in a contingency. On any construction project, unexpected things happen. Budget for them — typically 10-15% of your construction cost.
  • Get a detailed scope before you sign anything. A vague quote is not a quote. Insist on a breakdown by trade, by phase, and by specification. This protects you and makes it possible to compare quotes properly.
  • Understand what's included and what's not. "Construction cost" often excludes landscaping, pool, boundary walls, gate, air conditioning, kitchen appliances, and furniture. Make sure you know what you're comparing.
  • Don't optimise purely for the lowest number. In construction, the cheapest quote usually means something is missing — either from the scope, or from the quality of the work. The question isn't "how cheap can I build?" but "what am I getting for this price?"

The bottom line

Construction costs in Phuket are genuinely variable — more so than in most markets — because of the range of land conditions, design possibilities, material choices, and contractor quality you'll encounter. Anyone claiming to give you a reliable number without knowing your specific situation is not being straight with you.

What we can offer is an honest assessment based on your actual project. If you're at the early stages of thinking about building or renovating in Phuket, the most useful thing you can do is have a conversation with someone who will tell you the truth — including the parts you might not want to hear.
